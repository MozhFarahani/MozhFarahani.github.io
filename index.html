Applied ML scientist for land, water, and climate models.

I am a scientist at the National Center for Atmospheric Research. I combine machine learning with physics-based models: ML emulators that calibrate large-domain hydrology models, federated learning across regions, and information theory to test whether a model captures the right relationships and not only good scores. I take that research from idea to scalable, reproducible code on HPC, and my methods are now used in multi-agency projects. This page collects my research, code, publications, and the work I want to do next.

HESS 2025

ML-based calibration of a large-domain hydrology model

An AI-driven calibration workflow for the SUMMA process-based hydrology model. Machine-learning emulators are coupled with genetic-algorithm optimization (NSGA-II) to search high-dimensional parameter spaces across 627 CAMELS basins, refining estimates through successive emulator-guided model runs. Median streamflow accuracy (KGE′) rose from 0.30 to 0.76, compared with 0.69 for per-basin calibration, and spatial cross-validation tested how well the approach regionalizes to unseen basins.

The framework now extends to the larger CAMELS-X dataset with an improved optimization strategy, a global emulator, and a complementary regional emulator trained with federated learning (manuscript in preparation).

CMIP6 LOCA2 streamflow projections Ongoing

A multi-agency effort with the Bureau of Reclamation and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. I lead the calibration workstream: an end-to-end HPC pipeline that uses the trained emulators to generate parameters for ~97,000 HUC12 subbasins across the contiguous US, runs large simulation ensembles, and automates parameter selection gauge by gauge through the river network. I coordinate region-by-region delivery to partners.

For the project team, I built a Python and Plotly tool that turns HPC model output into interactive diagnostic dashboards, with CAMELS-X benchmarking, streamflow signatures, and automated failure flags, to find and diagnose poorly performing gauges.

Aug–Sep 2026

CTSM-CONUS: high-resolution land and river modeling

Which CTSM configuration choices matter most for fine-scale hydrology over the contiguous US? I ran the Community Terrestrial Systems Model (CTSM 5.4, CLM6 physics) coupled to the mizuRoute river model on the NLDAS-2 1/8° grid: 80,439 land cells, 227,247 MERIT-Hydro river reaches, and 38 water years of NLDAS-2 forcing. Each of five configurations changes one option from a control: irrigation, evolving land cover, hillslope hydrology, or prognostic phenology.

Routed streamflow was scored against 2,286 USGS, USACE, and USBR gauges (monthly NSE, KGE, percent bias). Hillslope hydrology is the only option that changes skill substantially, roughly doubling median NSE, with the largest gains across the West and the Missouri basin. The work is a two-month Science Innovation Fund (SIF-T) project, published as a project website, an interactive dashboard, a Jupyter Book technical note, and a code repository.

2025–2026

WANA Affinity Group: scientific collaboration across West Asia & North Africa

The WANA Affinity Group is a grassroots community, started in 2023, that connects UCAR, NSF NCAR, and UCP scientists who work in or have ties to the West Asia–North Africa region, where extreme heat, water scarcity, flash floods, and dust storms are intensifying. With a UCAR President’s Strategic Initiative Fund award, our team of twelve interviewed researchers and surveyed staff to build an open inventory of past and current WANA projects, covering cloud seeding, renewable-energy forecasting, dust and air quality, hydrology, S2S prediction, and public health.

The final report summarizes lessons learned on data sharing, realistic scoping, and building trust with partners, and identifies future priorities and funding paths. We presented the findings at a CGD seminar in August 2026.
