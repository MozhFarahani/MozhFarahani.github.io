Hydrology · Land modeling · Machine learning

Teaching process models to learn from every river gauge in the country.

I am an applied machine learning scientist at the National Center for Atmospheric Research. I combine machine learning with physics-based models: ML emulators that calibrate large-domain hydrology models, federated learning across regions, and information theory to test whether a model captures the right relationships and not only good scores. I take that research from idea to scalable, reproducible code on HPC, and my methods are now used in multi-agency projects. This page collects my research, code, publications, and the work I want to do next.

Research & projects

HESS 2025

ML-based calibration of a large-domain hydrology model

An AI-driven calibration workflow for the SUMMA process-based hydrology model. Machine-learning emulators are coupled with genetic-algorithm optimization (NSGA-II) to search high-dimensional parameter spaces across 627 CAMELS basins, refining estimates through successive emulator-guided model runs. Median streamflow accuracy (KGE′) rose from 0.30 to 0.76, compared with 0.69 for per-basin calibration, and spatial cross-validation tested how well the approach regionalizes to unseen basins.

The framework now extends to the larger CAMELS-X dataset with an improved optimization strategy, a global emulator, and a complementary regional emulator trained with federated learning (manuscript in preparation).

2026

CTSM-CONUS: high-resolution land and river modeling

A high-resolution CTSM hydrology evaluation and community baseline dataset for the contiguous US. It couples the Community Terrestrial Systems Model (CTSM 5.4) with the mizuRoute river model on a 0.125° (~12 km) grid of 224 × 464 cells, with 227,000 river reaches and 38 water years, to isolate how irrigation, evolving land cover, hillslope hydrology, and prognostic phenology affect streamflow.

A parallel Python pipeline scores five model configurations against 2,200+ stream gauges (NSE, KGE, percent bias) by region. I built the project website, an interactive dashboard of gauge maps, time series, seasonal climatologies, and configuration differences, and an online technical note. Case-creation scripts, the configuration archive, and automated tests are released as an open-source repository. Funded by an NCAR CGD Science Innovation Fund (SIF-T) award.

Future work & collaboration

ML emulators for CTSM calibration at CONUS scale

Seeking: land modelers, ML researchers

Carry the SUMMA emulator approach into CTSM-CONUS so a full land model can be calibrated against thousands of gauges, and test whether calibrated parameters transfer across configurations such as irrigation and hillslope hydrology.

Federated learning across agencies

Seeking: water agencies, data holders

Train regional hydrologic emulators where data cannot be pooled in one place, and measure how much a shared global model gains from regional partners.

Where does S2S streamflow skill come from?

Seeking: forecasters, DA groups

Use information-theory diagnostics to separate predictability carried by snowpack, soil moisture, and forcings, and connect that to data assimilation choices.

Open gauge-level benchmarking

Seeking: model developers

Turn the diagnostics dashboards into a shared benchmark so any model configuration can be scored against the same gauges, signatures, and failure flags.
